1Purpose and General Expectations

The purpose of this program is to ensure that every franchise location consistently meets company standards for food quality, cleanliness, customer service, food safety, operational procedures, and brand presentation.

Franchise ownership comes with the responsibility of maintaining the standards established by the company. While franchisees have the ability to manage their individual locations, that independence does not extend to making unauthorized changes to company products, procedures, or operating standards.

All franchisees and general managers are expected to:

  • Maintain a clean, organized, and professional restaurant environment.
  • Follow all company-approved recipes, preparation methods, portion sizes, and food-handling procedures.
  • Purchase and use only approved food products, packaging, paper products, and operating supplies.
  • Maintain appropriate food safety, sanitation, and employee hygiene standards.
  • Ensure employees are properly trained on company procedures.
  • Address customer complaints and operational concerns promptly.
  • Maintain all equipment and facilities in safe working condition.
  • Cooperate with corporate inspections, secret shoppers, and follow-up evaluations.
  • Correct deficiencies within the assigned timelines.
  • Communicate operational concerns and requests for exceptions through the appropriate corporate channels. (These will be updated or specified with request forms and a proper approval process/timeline.)
  • Ensure that all managers and employees understand and follow current company policies.
The franchisee is ultimately responsible for ensuring that these expectations are maintained, regardless of whether the franchisee is physically present at the location.

2Performance Standards and Minimum Scores

All franchise locations are expected to maintain a score above 80% in both secret shopper evaluations and quarterly corporate inspections.

A. Secret Shopper Program · 1 visit per location per month

Secret shoppers will evaluate the customer-facing experience and consistency of the brand across locations. Evaluations may include:

  • Customer service and employee professionalism.
  • Order accuracy.
  • Food quality and presentation.
  • Product temperature and freshness.
  • Cleanliness of dining areas and customer-facing spaces.
  • Speed of service.
  • Employee knowledge and attentiveness.
  • Overall customer experience.

A poor secret shopper score should be reviewed with the franchisee or general manager. The purpose is to identify specific deficiencies rather than simply communicate a failing grade.
B. Quarterly Corporate Inspections · once per quarter

Corporate will conduct a formal operational inspection at each franchise location once per quarter. Inspections will evaluate the overall condition and operation of the restaurant. Inspection categories will include:

🧼 1. Food Safety & Sanitation

  • Proper food storage and labeling.
  • Temperature logs and food temperatures.
  • Cross-contamination prevention.
  • Handwashing and employee hygiene.
  • Cleaning and sanitation procedures.
  • Pest prevention.

🍣 2. Food Quality & Preparation

  • Approved ingredients and products.
  • Proper recipes and preparation methods.
  • Portion control.
  • Product freshness and rotation.
  • Proper presentation.

🧹 3. Cleanliness & Maintenance

  • Kitchen and preparation areas.
  • Dining room and customer areas.
  • Restrooms.
  • Floors, walls, ceilings, and drains.
  • Equipment cleanliness and functionality.
  • Exterior appearance and signage.

📋 4. Operational Procedures

  • Opening and closing procedures.
  • Inventory management.
  • Employee training.
  • Approved equipment and supplies.
  • Proper use of company systems.
  • Compliance with current operating procedures.

⭐ 5. Management & Brand Standards

  • Management presence and accountability.

  • Uniform and appearance standards.

3Approved Products, Supplies, and Procedures

Maintaining consistency across all locations is essential to protecting the brand and customer experience. All food products, ingredients, paper products, packaging, cleaning supplies, equipment, and other designated operating materials must be approved by corporate before being used.

Franchisees may not independently substitute products or modify established procedures simply because another option is cheaper, more convenient, or more readily available.
A. Approved Products

Corporate will maintain an approved product and supplier list. The list should include:

  • Approved product name and description.
  • Approved brand or manufacturer.
  • Approved supplier.
  • Product specifications, where applicable.
  • Approved alternatives, if available.

Products that are not included on the approved list should be considered unauthorized until written approval is received.

B. Requesting a Product Substitution

Submit a Product Substitution Request Form before purchasing or using the proposed replacement. The request must include:

  • Current approved product.
  • Proposed replacement product.
  • Manufacturer and supplier.
  • Product specifications.
  • Pricing comparison, when applicable.
  • Reason for requesting the substitution.
  • Samples, photographs, or product documentation when requested.

Corporate will review the request based on quality, consistency, food safety, pricing, availability, and brand standards.

Approval must be received in writing before the replacement product is used. Submitting a request does not constitute approval.
C. Procedural Changes

The same approval process applies to changes involving:

  • Food preparation methods.
  • Cooking times and temperatures.
  • Portion sizes.
  • Storage and handling.
  • Cleaning procedures.
  • Opening and closing procedures.
  • Equipment usage.
  • Other established operating procedures.

No franchisee or general manager may independently alter company procedures. A Procedural Change Request Form must be submitted and approved before implementation, except where an immediate safety or legal requirement necessitates action. In those circumstances, corporate must be notified immediately.

D. Unauthorized Products and Procedures

The use of unauthorized products or intentional deviation from established procedures may result in:

  • An inspection violation.
  • A corrective action requirement.
  • An Out-of-Compliance Deviation Form.
  • A reinspection with a $300 charge.
  • Additional enforcement for repeat violations.

Once notified of an unauthorized product or procedure, the franchisee must discontinue the practice within the timeframe specified by corporate.

4Inspection Findings and Corrective Actions

When an inspection is completed, corporate will provide the franchisee or general manager with a written inspection report. The report will identify:

  • Overall inspection score.
  • Individual deficiencies.
  • Applicable company standards.
  • Required corrective actions.
  • Deadline for correction.
  • Whether a reinspection is required.

Each finding should be documented clearly enough that the franchisee understands what needs to be corrected.

A. Corrective Action Requirements

All identified deficiencies must be corrected within the assigned timeframe. Correction deadlines will depend on the severity of the violation.

Critical food safety or immediate safety issueImmediately
Unauthorized product or procedureImmediately discontinue; corrective action as directed
Cleanliness or sanitation deficiencyWithin 24 hours
Minor operational deficiencyWithin 7 days
Equipment or maintenance issueWithin 14 days, unless an approved extension is granted
Significant repair or facility improvementAs specified in an approved corrective action timeline

These are standard target timelines. Corporate may assign a shorter deadline when circumstances warrant it.

B. Reinspection Requirements

A location that fails its quarterly inspection will be subject to a follow-up reinspection. Corporate will determine the actual reinspection date within this window:

Reinspection window
Day 0: failed inspection≥ 48 hoursDay 14 (calendar)

$300  Reinspection Fee, charged to the franchisee for each required reinspection resulting from a failed inspection. The fee is intended to account for the additional corporate time and resources required to revisit a location that did not meet established standards.

C. Requirements Before Reinspection

Before a reinspection occurs, the franchisee must ensure that:

  1. All previously identified violations have been corrected.
  2. Any outstanding items are actively being addressed.
  3. All employees and managers have been informed of the deficiencies relevant to their responsibilities.
  4. Any approved extensions are documented.
  5. The location is prepared to demonstrate compliance with all previously identified standards.
A reinspection is not limited to checking only the items that previously failed. Corporate may review the entire location to verify that the original deficiencies have been corrected and that no new compliance issues have developed. All previously marked items must be corrected or actively in the process of being corrected.
Simply stating that an issue will be addressed is not sufficient. The franchisee must be able to demonstrate actual corrective action.
D. Repair and Extension Requests

Some repairs or operational improvements may reasonably require more than the standard correction period. Examples include:

  • Replacement of major equipment.
  • Contractor scheduling.
  • Plumbing or electrical repairs.
  • Facility improvements.
  • Special-order parts.
  • Significant structural repairs.

In these situations, the franchisee must submit a Corrective Action Extension Request Form directly to the Program Owner. The request must include:

  • Original inspection finding.
  • Reason the correction cannot be completed by the deadline.
  • Actions already taken.
  • Supporting documentation, when applicable.
  • Expected completion date.
  • Temporary measures being used to maintain compliance.
The request must be submitted and approved before the scheduled reinspection date. Corporate will determine whether an extension is reasonable and whether temporary corrective measures are sufficient. An extension is not automatically granted because a request has been submitted.
Extensions do not excuse immediate food safety or other critical violations. A location must remain safe and compliant while permanent repairs are pending.

5Repeat Violations and Escalation

Repeated violations demonstrate a failure to maintain company standards and require additional accountability. The purpose of escalation is to ensure that deficiencies are permanently corrected rather than repeatedly addressed only when corporate is scheduled to visit.

Tip: use the Escalation stepper at the top of this page to step through each level.

A. First Failed Inspection

  • Written inspection report issued.
  • Corrective action requirements provided.
  • Reinspection scheduled within the required 14-day window.
  • $300 reinspection fee applied.

B. Second Failed Inspection / Repeat Violations

If a location fails its reinspection or the same violations are identified again:

  • A second $300 reinspection fee will apply if another reinspection is required.
  • A formal Corrective Action Plan must be submitted by the franchisee.
  • Corporate may require additional management training.
  • A second follow-up inspection may be scheduled within 7 days, or another reasonable timeframe established by corporate.
  • Repeated violations may result in an Out-of-Compliance Deviation Form.

C. Third Failed Inspection / Continued Noncompliance

  • Corporate may require a formal meeting with the franchisee.
  • A written improvement plan with specific milestones may be required.
  • Additional inspections may be conducted.
  • The franchisee may be required to demonstrate management competency and retrain employees.
  • Further fees may apply for additional reinspections.
  • Corporate may initiate formal enforcement procedures under the franchise agreement.

D. Persistent or Serious Noncompliance

Subject to the franchise agreement and applicable law. Potential consequences include:

  • Formal notices of default.
  • Required operational supervision or additional oversight.
  • Restrictions on unauthorized business practices.
  • Required corrective training.
  • Additional contractual remedies.
  • Suspension or termination of franchise rights when legally and contractually justified.
The Corrective Action Plan must identify the underlying reason for the repeated issue, the responsible party, the corrective action, and the expected completion date.
Serious food safety issues, intentional violations, or conduct that creates an immediate risk to customers or employees may warrant immediate intervention rather than waiting for the normal escalation process.

6Out-of-Compliance Deviation Form

The Out-of-Compliance Deviation Form is intended to document situations where a franchisee or general manager knowingly continues to violate an established company standard after being informed that the practice is not permitted. This is different from an ordinary inspection finding.

🔎 Inspection Finding

Documents a deficiency.

⚠️ Deviation Form

Documents an intentional departure from an established requirement, particularly when the individual has already received notice and an opportunity to correct the issue.
When to Issue a Deviation Form
  • Continuing to purchase or use an unauthorized food product after being instructed to stop.
  • Repeatedly using an unapproved ingredient substitution.
  • Intentionally disregarding a required food preparation procedure.
  • Refusing to correct an identified sanitation issue.
  • Knowingly operating equipment or processes contrary to company requirements.
  • Repeatedly failing to follow an approved corrective action plan.
Required Documentation
  • Location and franchisee.
  • Date of violation.
  • Individual responsible, if known.
  • Company standard or procedure violated.
  • Date the original issue was communicated.
  • Previous corrective actions or warnings.
  • Description of the continued deviation.
  • Supporting evidence, such as photographs or inspection reports.
  • Required corrective action.
  • Deadline for compliance.
  • Franchisee acknowledgment.
  • Corporate representative signature.
The franchisee's signature acknowledges receipt of the notice, not necessarily agreement with its contents. A franchisee may provide a written explanation or dispute the finding. Corporate should retain that response with the documentation.
Intentional deviations may trigger accelerated escalation, even if the location's overall inspection score remains above 80%.

7General Franchisee and General Manager Expectations

Every franchisee is responsible for ensuring that their location operates consistently with company standards. The franchisee may delegate daily responsibilities to a general manager, but delegation does not remove the franchisee's responsibility for compliance.

👔 Franchisee Responsibilities

  • Maintain all company operating standards.
  • Ensure adequate management coverage.
  • Provide employees with appropriate training.
  • Purchase approved products and supplies.
  • Maintain facilities and equipment.
  • Respond to corporate communications.
  • Address inspection findings within established deadlines.
  • Submit requests for exceptions before making changes.
  • Ensure that reinspection requirements are met.
  • Maintain accurate operational records.
  • Ensure that the general manager understands corporate expectations.

🧑‍🍳 General Manager Responsibilities

  • Maintain daily operational standards.
  • Ensure employees follow established procedures.
  • Monitor food quality and food safety.
  • Maintain cleanliness throughout the location.
  • Conduct regular internal checks.
  • Identify and communicate maintenance concerns.
  • Ensure employees are trained.
  • Correct issues as they arise.
  • Communicate corporate inspection findings to the appropriate employees.
  • Follow through on assigned corrective actions.
Communication Expectations

Corporate should be notified promptly of issues that may affect:

  • Food safety.
  • Customer safety.
  • Product availability.
  • Equipment functionality.
  • Significant facility repairs.
  • Operational continuity.
  • Brand reputation.
Franchisees should not wait until an inspection to report significant operational concerns.

11Program Administration and Recordkeeping

Corporate Operations will maintain a compliance record for each franchise location.

🗂️ Each location's file should contain

  • Quarterly inspection reports.
  • Monthly secret shopper scorecards.
  • Reinspection reports.
  • Reinspection fee records.
  • Product substitution requests and decisions.
  • Procedural change requests and decisions.
  • Corrective action extension requests.
  • Out-of-Compliance Deviation Forms.
  • Corrective Action Plans.
  • Relevant communications and supporting documentation.

📊 Recommended tracking metrics

  • Current quarterly inspection score.
  • Previous quarterly inspection scores.
  • Monthly secret shopper scores.
  • Rolling three-month secret shopper average.
  • Number of reinspections.
  • Total reinspection fees.
  • Outstanding corrective actions.
  • Repeat violations.
  • Unauthorized product or procedure incidents.
  • Open extension requests.

Corporate should also maintain a centralized performance tracker that allows management to compare locations and identify recurring issues across the franchise system. The program should be reviewed annually to ensure that expectations remain consistent, enforceable, and aligned with the company's operating standards.

Final Standard: Every franchise location is expected to maintain the same level of quality, safety, cleanliness, and customer experience that the company expects from its corporate-operated locations. Franchise ownership does not remove the responsibility to protect the brand and maintain established operating standards.
